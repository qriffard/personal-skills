# Full style profile — Quentin Riffard

Derived from first-author/corresponding-author corpus: arXiv 1602.01738, 1602.01030, 1504.05865, 1306.4173, 1801.06653 (ARIS), PhD thesis 2015 (French, tel-01258830), Berkeley Lab Dark Matter Day Q&A (2019).

## 1. Voice & tone

- Collective first person dominates: "we" even for solo work — "we proposed an original method", "we can note that", "we have shown". Passive for apparatus/procedure ("the detector was weekly calibrated", "data were acquired"), active "we" for analysis decisions and claims. ~60/40 we-active vs passive in methods; conclusions almost entirely "we have shown/presented/measured".
- Reader-guiding "we": author as reader-companion — "we can clearly identify two peaks", "we can observe that", "we can note that", "It can be seen that". The "we can + perception verb" framing is the single most distinctive tic.
- Hedge vocabulary (exact words): "should" (heavily), "could", "roughly", "about", "in general", "in a first (order) approximation", "may be", "expected to". Hedges predictions, assertive about measurements: "This measurement shows the capability of the detector", "This analysis shows that a rejection power is reachable".
- Assertive closure: "In conclusion, …" mid-section, closing sub-arguments explicitly: "In conclusion, the neutron field can be considered as a monochromatic neutron field."
- Novelty claims direct and repeated: "for the first time", "an original method", "an original detector and readout strategy", "opens the possibility to explore".

## 2. Sentence & paragraph architecture

- Medium-long sentences (20–35 words) with a subordinate tail: main claim first, then participial or "which/that" clause carrying mechanism ("These electrons are drifted to the grid by an electric field, reaching the pixelized anode").
- Purpose-first openers: "In order to + infinitive" ("In order to evaluate the electron event rejection, data-taking with and without…"). Also "For [X], …", "In this context, …", "In the case of …".
- Paragraphs context-first, claim-last: 1–2 sentences of setup, then observation, then one-sentence interpretation.
- Transition/connector inventory (verbatim, high frequency): "Indeed," (signature — justifies the previous sentence, calqued from "En effet"), "Moreover,", "Thus,", "Hence,", "In addition,", "However,", "First, … Second, … Finally/Eventually,", "Then,", "Furthermore,", "Note that", "We can note that", "As mentioned/discussed before/above", "As shown in/on".
- Section openers restate the goal ("In this section, a boosted decision trees analysis strategy and the results obtained will be presented") or define the object under study. Intros end with an explicit roadmap: "First, in section I we recall… In section III, … Finally, we present in section VI…".

## 3. Technical presentation

- Numbers: value ± uncertainty with unit, "16 % at 3 keV", ranges as "[7, 120] keV", "ranging from 5.5 to 8.8 MeV", "from 38 to 70 keV". Approximations: "roughly 57 keV", "about 40%", "of the order of". Units meticulously distinguished (keV/keVee/keVnr).
- Figures/tables: "Figure 4 shows…", "as shown in figure 1", "see table 2". Figures are active subjects: "This figure highlights the differences…", "These spectra show two peaks at roughly 33 and 45 keVee."
- Methods narrated as temporal chain: "First, … Next, … Then, … Finally, …", heavy tool naming with citations immediately after.
- Results vs interpretation separated: measurement sentence, then interpretation sentence beginning "It indicates/confirms/implies that…", "This is consistent with…", "supporting our statement". Definitions formalized: "The rejection power … is defined as the ratio between…".
- Parentheticals: acronym expansion at first use — "the WIMP (Weakly Interacting Massive Particle)"; "(resp. neutron)" for paired quantities; "i.e." and "e.g." inline; short clarifiers "(red line)", "(52% of the total)".

## 4. Structure habits

- Abstract: 4–6 sentences — context (1), what was done ("In this paper, we proposed/report…"), quantitative headline result, "Moreover, …" secondary result, forward-looking significance ("opens the possibility to…", "constitutes a key issue for…").
- Intro: broad-to-specific funnel: universal context, the hypothesis/strategy, the specific problem, then "In this paper, we…" + section roadmap.
- Conclusions: recap "In this paper, we have presented/shown…", restate the key number, then "The next step will be…" / "will be the subject of a future/dedicated paper" — end on perspectives, never on limitations alone.

## 5. Lexical fingerprint

Favorite verbs/constructions: *allow* ("allowed us to obtain", "allows the measurement of", "allows one to"), *present* ("the distribution presents two peaks"), *show/shows that*, *perform* ("a scan was performed"), *highlight*, *exploit*, *constrain*, *assess*.

Verbatim templates:
- "In this paper, we proposed an original method for…"
- "In order to evaluate…, data-taking … were performed."
- "We can clearly identify two peaks at…"
- "Indeed, this figure shows that…"
- "Note that we also checked that…"
- "It can first be noticed that…"
- "This measurement shows the capability of the MIMAC detector."
- "As mentioned before, …"
- "This will be the subject of a future paper."
- "For the sake of completeness, we consider…"

Never does: rhetorical questions in English papers; contractions; first-person singular "I" in papers; em-dash asides; bullet-heavy prose (bullets only for enumerated cut/event definitions); superlatives beyond "the most precise", "the first".

## 6. French-specific patterns (thesis)

- Same "we"-guide voice via "nous" ("Nous pouvons observer que", "Nous pouvons en conclure que", "Nous allons donc…"), except conclusion chapter, which switches to "j'ai montré / j'ai pu montrer" to claim personal contributions.
- Explicit forward announcements: "Dans ce chapitre, nous commencerons par… Puis… Enfin, nous étudierons…" — every chapter/section opens with a mini-plan; futures ("nous verrons par la suite") abound.
- Favored connectors: "Ainsi," (extremely frequent, opens conclusive sentences), "En effet,", "De plus,", "Cependant,", "Par ailleurs,", "Dans un second temps,", "À l'heure actuelle,", "Quant à X, …", "d'ores et déjà", "Il est important de noter que", "Il est aisé de montrer que", "Se posent alors deux grandes questions :".
- Allows a rhetorical question as pivot: "La question que nous pouvons maintenant nous poser est : … ? Pour y répondre, nous nous proposons de…" — "nous nous proposons de" is a signature.
- Definitions via "tel(le) que" chains: "telle que représentée sur la figure 2.13", "défini tel que :".

## 7. Non-native tells

Preserve (voice-defining):
- "Indeed," as justification connector.
- "we can + observe/note/see/conclude" reader-guidance; "Note that…".
- "allows one to / allows the measurement of"; "(resp. …)" pairing; "In order to" openers; "In conclusion," inside sections.
- "an original method/observable" meaning "novel".

Smooth (errors, not style — silently correct):
- Article slips (missing/extra "the").
- Agreement/number slips ("put constrains on", "must overestimates").
- "allows to + infinitive" without object → "allows us to / allows keeping".
- "instead of" used for "whereas".
- Faux amis: "performant", "the complementary of" → "complementarity", "comes from that" → "comes from the fact that".
- Comma splices joining observation+interpretation → split or use "Indeed,"/semicolon.
